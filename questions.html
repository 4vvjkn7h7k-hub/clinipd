SAMPLE Free

A 24-year-old recreational runner reports anterior knee pain worse on stairs and after prolonged sitting. Which finding would most increase your suspicion of patellofemoral pain over patellar tendinopathy?

A  Tenderness at the inferior pole of the patella
B  Pain reproduced by prolonged sitting with the knee flexed
C  Pain on single-leg decline squat
D  Morning stiffness lasting over an hour

B. The "movie sign" — pain on prolonged knee flexion — is characteristic of patellofemoral pain and is not a feature of patellar tendinopathy, which is load-related and typically eases with rest. Inferior pole tenderness (A) and decline squat pain (C) both point toward the tendon. Prolonged morning stiffness (D) suggests an inflammatory arthropathy.
